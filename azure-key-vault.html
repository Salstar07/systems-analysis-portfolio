01 / THE PROBLEM

What needed
to change?

A protected credential store is useful only when applications can consume secrets safely and authorised support personnel can recover service without creating excessive permanent privileges.

02 / THE CONTRIBUTION

What I worked on.

Conducted security assessment and hands-on sandbox validation, including protected runtime access and a bounded synthetic-secret recovery rehearsal. Documented control results, exceptions and recommended operational boundaries for management review.

SCOPE OF CONTRIBUTION

These accounts distinguish work I contributed from wider team outcomes and explain which results were actually demonstrated. Original employer and university records remain with their authorised owners.

03 / THE JUDGEMENT

Decisions that mattered.

01

Eliminate secrets when possible

Prefer managed identity or supported federation where credentials are unnecessary.

02

Separate runtime and operator authority

A consuming application needs only its data role, while lifecycle repair and access administration require distinct approval.

03

Prove recovery, not merely configuration

Exercise a controlled rotate/recover/regrant path and preserve traceable audit evidence and limits.

05 / OUTCOME & LIMITS

What I can
defend.

REPORTED OUTCOME

The adoption approach for residual machine credentials was accepted following the technical assessment. This was an operating-direction decision—not verification of a production vault deployment or unrestricted credential migration.

BOUNDARY / LIMITATION

The controlled rehearsal did not prove every disaster-recovery scenario or a fully reduced-privilege operational staff role. Sensitive tenant, network and control specifics are intentionally excluded.

WHAT COMES NEXT

Translate the accepted approach into workload-specific operational records, approvals and scoped deployment controls.
