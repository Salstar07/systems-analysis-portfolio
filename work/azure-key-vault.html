01 / BUSINESS PROBLEM

Secure access.
Recoverable operations.

A protected credential store must do more than hold secrets. Applications need appropriately scoped access, and authorised staff need a workable recovery path without giving every consuming workload broad administrative authority.

Project objectives

  • Evaluate a suitable approach for residual machine credentials.
  • Test identity, access, private-network consumption and selected recovery paths in a sandbox.
  • Give management a recommendation supported by test evidence, risks and operating requirements.
02 / MY CONTRIBUTION

Where I contributed.

I conducted the technical assessment and hands-on sandbox validation, including protected runtime access and a bounded recovery rehearsal with synthetic secrets. I documented results, exceptions and recommended operational boundaries for management review.

  • Assessed managed identities, RBAC, private networking and the separation of runtime and operator privileges.
  • Performed controlled access checks and synthetic rotation, soft-delete/recovery and identity-rebind exercises.
  • Recorded audit evidence, operating considerations, cost evidence and follow-up items for the recommendation.
SCOPE OF CONTRIBUTION

The technical proof of concept and the accepted adoption approach are distinct from a production deployment. Sensitive tenant, network and security configuration is excluded.

03 / REQUIREMENTS

Evaluate the operating model,
as well as the service.

The assessment considered who consumes a secret, who can administer access, and who can restore service when an identity or credential changes. The evaluation connected those responsibilities to role boundaries, auditability and recovery evidence.

  • Least privilege. Separate secret consumption from lifecycle repair and access administration.
  • Traceability. Retain useful evidence of the bounded access and recovery exercises.
  • Operational recovery. Examine a controlled recovery path and record what it does not establish.
04 / SOLUTION DESIGN

An identity-based
proof of concept.

A controlled Azure Key Vault and Azure Functions proof of concept examined managed-identity access, role-bound permissions and private-network consumption. A synthetic-secret rehearsal exercised rotation, soft-delete/recovery, identity rebind and audit retrieval without exposing operational credentials.

Decisions & trade-offs

01

Avoid a secret where identity can replace it

Prefer managed identity or supported federation when the workload can authenticate without storing an additional credential.

02

Separate runtime from operator authority

A consuming application needs its scoped data role. Recovery and access administration need separately controlled authority and operational ownership.

03

Rehearse the recovery path

Configuration alone does not establish recoverability. The bounded exercise provided evidence for selected steps while leaving wider disaster-recovery scenarios open.

05 / IMPLEMENTATION

Putting the approach
into practice.

I carried out the controlled Key Vault and Azure Functions proof of concept, using synthetic secrets for the recovery rehearsal. The work included managed-identity access, scoped role checks and recording audit evidence for management review; it did not constitute a production rollout.

06 / TESTING

What was checked.

The existing assessment account reports successful private-network consumption and role-bound access checks. It also records a passed bounded rehearsal covering synthetic rotation, soft-delete/recovery, identity rebind and audit retrieval. These reported results are confined to the evaluated sandbox path.

08 / OUTCOMES

What the work
establishes.

The adoption approach for residual machine credentials was accepted following the successful technical assessment. The work supported an operating-direction decision with explicit implementation follow-up.

09 / LIMITATIONS

Where the evidence
stops.

This does not verify a production vault deployment, unrestricted credential migration or every disaster-recovery scenario. A fully reduced-privilege operational staff role was not established by the controlled rehearsal.
