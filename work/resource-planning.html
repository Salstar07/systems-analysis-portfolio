01 / BUSINESS PROBLEM & OBJECTIVES

Planning starts with
reliable information.

Resource decisions bring together project scope, fee-hour demand, employee availability and existing allocations. Missing or inconsistent source records can make a planning interface appear more certain than the evidence allows.

Project objectives

  • Make availability, planned work and gaps in source data understandable to planners.
  • Translate business rules into consistent application behaviour and acceptance checks.
  • Keep valid projects visible while reconciling incomplete fee or demand information.
02 / MY CONTRIBUTION

Where I contributed.

At CivilSE, I contributed to business and technical requirements analysis, Power Apps Code App development and refinement, and investigation of planning and data-quality defects. My focus connected the intended workflow to the behaviour observed in the application.

  • Clarified availability, allocation and project-discovery rules for the planning workflow.
  • Worked on React/TypeScript application refinements backed by Dataverse and Microsoft business systems.
  • Investigated filtering, allocation and Xero-related source-data reconciliation issues; documented expected behaviour and follow-up checks.
SCOPE OF CONTRIBUTION

The planner is described at development and user acceptance testing (UAT) stage. This account covers my analysis, refinement and verification contribution within the wider project.

03 / REQUIREMENTS & ANALYSIS

Start with the rule.
Then trace the data.

I examined the relationship between projects, source records, availability and planned work, and translated ambiguous behaviours into requirements that could be checked. Reconciliation depended on authoritative records and stable identifiers rather than display names or assumed values.

  • Data completeness. Treat missing availability or fee information as unknown; show the gap before presenting a full aggregate.
  • Consistent identity. Reconcile projects and planning values against their source records and relationships.
  • Usable discovery. Investigate why a valid project is absent or filtered out before treating it as having no demand.
04 / TECHNICAL APPROACH

Application behaviour
grounded in sources.

The project uses Power Apps Code Apps with React and TypeScript, Dataverse, Power Automate and SharePoint. My development and investigation work connected interface behaviour to planning rules and the data supplied through those business systems, including Xero-related integration analysis.

05 / DECISIONS & TRADE-OFFS

Decisions that mattered.

01

Keep unknown separate from zero

A missing value should remain visible as a data-quality issue. This can withhold a convenient total, but avoids implying that incomplete records describe confirmed capacity.

02

Preserve visibility while reconciling

A valid project should remain discoverable when fee or demand details need investigation. Data completeness and project existence are separate questions.

03

Use source-backed comparisons

Stable identities and authoritative source records support reconciliation; display labels and inferred figures are insufficient evidence.
