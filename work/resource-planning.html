01 / THE PROBLEM

What needed
to change?

Planning decisions depend on project scope, fee-hour demand, employee capacity and the relationship between them. When source information is missing or inconsistent, an application risks displaying deceptively certain results.

02 / THE CONTRIBUTION

What I worked on.

Contributed to clarifying planning rules, refining an application backed by Microsoft business systems, tracing data-quality issues, and investigating allocation, filtering and reconciliation defects. Work included translating expected behaviour into testable checks.

SCOPE OF CONTRIBUTION

These accounts distinguish work I contributed from wider team outcomes and explain which results were actually demonstrated. Original employer and university records remain with their authorised owners.

03 / THE JUDGEMENT

Decisions that mattered.

01

Distinguish unknown from zero

Missing availability or fee information must not quietly become a confirmed zero. User-facing totals should expose incomplete evidence.

02

Preserve project visibility

Valid projects remain discoverable even when their fee or demand data needs reconciliation.

03

Trace to authoritative sources

Planning values are compared with source records and stable identities, rather than relying on display names or inferred figures.

05 / OUTCOME & LIMITS

What I can
defend.

REPORTED OUTCOME

A structured development and verification basis for resource-planning functionality. The available working record describes active development/UAT, not a verified organisation-wide production outcome or financial saving.

BOUNDARY / LIMITATION

The independent data-rule demonstration is not the CivilSE application or historical proof of its implementation. Deployment state and user acceptance must be confirmed before making stronger claims.

WHAT COMES NEXT

Complete source-data reconciliation, owner-led acceptance and baseline-versus-after measurement before reporting business benefits.
